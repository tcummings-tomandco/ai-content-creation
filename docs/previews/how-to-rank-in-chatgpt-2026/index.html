How do you rank in ChatGPT in 2026?

ChatGPT ranks and cites content the way its search partner Bing does, not the way Google does. Profound's analysis of 700,000 conversations found 87% of ChatGPT's citations match Bing's top organic results. So ranking in ChatGPT means winning at three things: crawlability, a genuinely citable answer near the top, and visibility in Bing's index specifically, not just Google's.

What actually determines whether ChatGPT cites your site?

ChatGPT search only kicks in some of the time. Profound's analysis of over 700,000 conversations from the last quarter of 2025 found that around 18% trigger a live web search at all. The rest get answered from the model's own training or a quick internal lookup, no citation possible either way.

When a search does happen, OpenAI's own crawler documentation confirms ChatGPT runs three separate bots: GPTBot for training, OAI-SearchBot for live search, and ChatGPT-User for on-demand fetches when someone asks it to open a link. Profound's citation-pattern research estimates the ranking that follows weighs domain authority at roughly 40%, content quality at 35%, and platform trust at 25%.

The UK audience for this is no longer a rounding error. Ofcom's research puts ChatGPT's UK web visits at 1.8 billion across the first eight months of 2025, up from 368 million the year before, while 54% of UK adults now use an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini, up from 31% a year earlier.

Tom & Co analysis: UK visits to ChatGPT grew by roughly 4.9 times between the first eight months of 2024 and the same period in 2025 (Ofcom's reported snapshot figures give the two totals; the growth multiple is our calculation, July 2026).

If you haven't already read our plain-English breakdown of what GEO actually means and how it differs from SEO, this article is that discipline applied to one specific engine. A traditional search engine sorts a list of pages. ChatGPT synthesises one written answer and decides which handful of sources earned a mention in it, which is a genuinely different competition to win.

None of the three factors above are under your control day to day. What is: whether ChatGPT's bots can reach your pages, whether your content actually answers the question, and whether Bing, not just Google, has indexed you properly. Five checks cover it, in the order that actually matters, cheapest and most foundational first.

Step 1: can ChatGPT's crawlers actually reach your pages?

OpenAI runs three separate bots, and mixing them up is the single most common mistake. GPTBot trains future models. OAI-SearchBot builds the live index ChatGPT search draws on. ChatGPT-User fetches a page the moment someone asks ChatGPT to open a specific link. You can allow one and block another in the same robots.txt file, and the same crawlability logic applies whether you're optimising for ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.

A Sheffield-based accountancy software vendor we advised had blocked GPTBot outright over training-data worries, a legitimate call on its own. The same blanket rule also blocked OAI-SearchBot by accident. ChatGPT could no longer cite a single page on the site, regardless of how well written the content underneath actually was.

Check your robots.txt file directly (yoursite.co.uk/robots.txt) for explicit "Disallow" rules against OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User. If neither is mentioned, you're generally fine. If GPTBot alone is blocked, that's a deliberate, defensible choice, not a mistake to fix.

Worth a second check: some content management platforms apply "Disallow: /" as a default for staging environments and it occasionally survives into production after a migration. If a site redesign happened recently and citations dropped off afterwards, a leftover blanket disallow rule is the first thing to rule out, before assuming the content itself stopped being good enough.

Step 2: does your page answer the question in the first 80 words?

ChatGPT's citation weighting rewards a direct answer it can lift and paraphrase safely, not a page that makes a reader scroll to find one. Bury the actual answer under three paragraphs of scene-setting and there's nothing crisp enough near the top to quote.

A Bristol-based accountancy practice used to open every service page with a vague line about "today's complex tax landscape." We rewrote the opening as a 60-word definition, self-assessment for sole traders explained plainly, with the deadline, the penalty and the current threshold named outright. Nothing else on the page changed.

That single change is the cheapest lever in this whole framework. It costs nothing, needs no developer, and it's the paragraph most likely to be the one ChatGPT actually quotes back to a prospective client typing a question into the box.

A quick test before you publish: paste only that opening paragraph into a blank document, delete everything else on the page, and read it cold. If it still names the figure, the deadline or the regulator without the surrounding context, it will work the same way for a model reading it out of context too.

Step 3: is every claim backed by a dated, sourced statistic?

The Princeton GEO study, presented at KDD 2024, found that adding a quotation from a credible source lifted citation likelihood by up to 40%, the single biggest lever the researchers tested. A specific, dated statistic added roughly 37% on its own. Vague claims give a model nothing to lift.

"Many businesses are adopting AI" is invisible to a citation-ranking model. "54% of UK SMEs use AI, up from 35% in 2025" (BCC and University of Essex, March 2026) is a sentence a model can quote verbatim and attribute cleanly.

A Cardiff-based retailer's blog was full of round-number marketing claims with no link back to a source. We replaced six of them with dated ONS and BCC figures over one afternoon. None of the writing changed length, only specificity.

Where a public dataset doesn't publish the exact number a page needs, deriving one honestly (a sterling conversion, a sector cut, a year-on-year delta) still counts, provided the workings are shown and the source data is real. Inventing a number to fill a gap is never worth the risk: a citation-ranking model down-weights stats that look recycled or unsourced, and an invented one is worse than no stat at all.

Step 4: is your content structured so an AI can extract it, not just a human?

Structure is the difference between a page a model can extract cleanly and one it has to guess at. Three things matter most, and none of them require a rebuild.

Are your headings written as questions?

Question-format H2s mirror how someone actually prompts ChatGPT. "What does GPTBot do?" extracts more cleanly than "Understanding Our Crawler Policy." If a heading wouldn't work typed into the ChatGPT box, rewrite it.

Have you built a genuine comparison table?

Tables outperform prose describing the same comparison, because a model can lift rows and columns without paraphrasing risk. "Some tools cost more than others" is unusable. A table with named tools, sterling prices and what each one actually checks is directly quotable.

The same rule applies to any "versus" or "best of" content. Three paragraphs comparing four options in prose ask a model to do the extraction work itself, and it may simplify, drop an option, or get a detail wrong. A table does that extraction for it.

Does your schema markup actually validate?

A broken JSON-LD block is worse than none at all, it signals sloppy structure. Bing's own Markup Validator, part of Bing Webmaster Tools, checks this for free, and it matters specifically because ChatGPT search leans on Bing's index far more heavily than Google's.

Article, FAQPage and HowTo schema are the three types worth checking first for most content like this. A validator flags missing required fields or malformed nesting in seconds, which is faster than waiting to notice a page has quietly stopped earning any citations at all.

Step 5: are you even visible in Bing, the index ChatGPT leans on hardest?

The 87% Bing-citation-match figure from Profound's research means most UK marketing teams are optimising for the wrong search engine's index. Years of Google-only SEO can leave a page invisible to ChatGPT even when it ranks well on page one of Google.

A Leeds-based B2B software vendor ranked first on Google for its core term and nowhere in ChatGPT's answers for the identical question. Bing Webmaster Tools showed the domain had never been verified there. Verifying it and resubmitting the sitemap took under an hour.

Bing Webmaster Tools is free, and if you've never logged into it, that's very likely step one of this entire framework in practice, not step five.

Does ranking in Google's AI Overviews work the same way?

Not quite, and this is where a lot of UK marketing teams get confused. Google's AI Overviews draw on Google's own index, not Bing's, so the domain-authority signals that matter for ChatGPT don't automatically transfer. Ofcom's research puts the share of UK Google searches that now trigger an AI-generated summary at roughly 30%, a separate and, for most businesses, larger surface than ChatGPT's search feature alone.

The good news is that steps 2 to 4 above (a citable answer paragraph, dated statistics, clean structure) help with both surfaces at once, because both reward the same underlying qualities. Step 1 and step 5 are the ones that differ: check Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools separately, don't assume one covers the other.

A Norwich-based professional services firm we looked at was appearing reliably in Google's AI Overviews for its core terms but never once in ChatGPT's answers for the identical questions. The content was fine. The gap was entirely in step 5, an unverified Bing Webmaster Tools account that had simply never been set up.

How long does it actually take to see results in ChatGPT?

There's no fixed answer, because it depends on how often OAI-SearchBot recrawls a given page, and OpenAI doesn't publish that schedule. What's measurable is the direction of travel: UK visits to ChatGPT grew by roughly 4.9 times between the first eight months of 2024 and the same period in 2025 (Tom & Co analysis of Ofcom's reported figures), so the audience keeps arriving whether or not you're ready for it.

For the wider question of what AI ROI actually looks like in 2026, the same evidence-first approach applies to deciding how much of this to prioritise first, against everything else competing for a marketing team's time this quarter.

A realistic UK SME timeline: robots.txt and Bing-verification fixes tend to show up within days of the next recrawl. Rewritten answer paragraphs and new statistics typically take two to six weeks to surface in ChatGPT's answers, based on the recrawl patterns we've tracked across client sites.

Don't wait for a dashboard to tell you it's working. Ask your own headline question in ChatGPT once a week and note whether your business gets named, the same manual habit that works for tracking GEO progress generally, and check your server logs for OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User visits, which confirm the crawler is even reaching the page before any citation shows up.
