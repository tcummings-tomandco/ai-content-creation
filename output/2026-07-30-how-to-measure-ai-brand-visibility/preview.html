How do you measure AI brand visibility in 2026?

Measuring AI brand visibility means tracking how often ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews name your business when someone asks a relevant question, not how you rank on a results page. Ahrefs' analysis of 3,000 sites found 63% already receive AI-referred traffic, up 9.7 times year-on-year. Five checks cover it: prompt testing, citation tracking, referral analytics, share-of-voice benchmarking and a content plan built from the gaps.

What is AI visibility and why does it need its own metric?

AI visibility is whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews mention your business by name when a prospective customer asks a relevant question. It is a citation contest, not a rankings one. A page can sit on page one of Google and never once get quoted inside an AI answer.

The audience behind this is no longer trivial. Ofcom's research puts UK web visits to ChatGPT at 1.8 billion across the first eight months of 2025, up from 368 million the year before, while 54% of UK adults now use an AI tool such as ChatGPT, Copilot or Gemini.

Ahrefs' analysis of an anonymised sample of 3,000 sites found 63% already receive at least some AI-referred traffic, and that traffic grew 9.7 times over the twelve months to June 2025. Most marketing dashboards still do not have a column for it.

(The awkward bit: most analytics platforms lump AI referrals in with "direct" traffic by default, so the true number is probably higher than any dashboard currently shows.) That is the whole reason this needs its own tracking process, not a bolt-on to existing SEO reporting.

A Nottingham-based homeware retailer we looked at ranked on page one of Google for its core category term, and had never once been named in ChatGPT's answer to the identical question. The website was fine. Nobody in the marketing team had ever actually asked ChatGPT the question and checked.

The commercial case is straightforward even without a perfect measurement system. A prospective buyer who reads a confident, well-sourced answer naming three suppliers has already shortened their own shortlist before a single ad or email reaches them. Not being one of the three named suppliers is a lost opportunity a traditional funnel report will never show.

Five checks build a full measurement process: a prompt list to test against, citation tracking on each engine, referral traffic in your own analytics, a share-of-voice comparison against named competitors, and a content plan built from whatever the first four checks turn up. Run in that order, cheapest and most revealing first.

Step 1: how do you build a prompt list that mirrors real customer questions?

Start with the questions people actually type, not the keywords you would have used for Google. Pull the last twenty search queries that reached your site, the phrasing from your top support tickets, and the way a colleague outside marketing would ask ChatGPT the same thing.

A realistic starting list runs to 15 to 30 prompts: your brand name alone, your brand alongside a named competitor, a "best X for Y" style question, and two or three problem-first questions that never mention any brand at all. Princeton's GEO study, presented at ACM SIGKDD 2024, found specific, well-sourced answers won citations up to 40% more often than vague ones.

Group the list by intent: brand-awareness prompts, comparison prompts, and category prompts where you are not named yet. The third group is usually the most revealing one, since it shows whether AI engines think of you at all before you tell them to.

A Liverpool-based logistics firm built its first list from eighteen months of live chat transcripts rather than starting from scratch. Customers had already typed the exact questions worth testing, in their own words, long before anyone thought to track AI visibility at all.

Refresh the list every quarter. New competitors enter the category, seasonal questions shift, and a prompt that returned nothing six months ago may now return a confident, well-cited answer once an engine has recrawled the relevant pages.

Step 2: how do you check whether AI engines are citing your business?

Run each prompt on each engine by hand and log whether your business is named, how it is described, and which URL, if any, gets cited. The three engines behave differently enough that treating them the same wastes the exercise.

ChatGPT

ChatGPT only triggers a live web search on a portion of conversations, so the same prompt can return a cited answer one day and a purely conversational one the next. Test each prompt on at least three separate days before drawing a conclusion.

Perplexity

Perplexity searches the live web by default on almost every query, which makes it the fastest engine to test against and the clearest early signal of whether your content is indexed for AI retrieval at all.

Gemini and Google's AI Overviews

Google's AI Overviews draw on Google's own index rather than Bing's, so strong Google SEO tends to transfer here more directly than it does to ChatGPT. Track the two separately, since a mention in one never guarantees the other.

Manual testing like this is free and it is the right place to start. The tools compared further down automate the same three checks on a schedule, once the manual version has proven worth paying for.

Log every result in a simple spreadsheet: date, engine, prompt, cited yes or no, the URL cited if any, and a one-line note on how your business was described. An Edinburgh-based fintech vendor kept this exact log for four months before a single pattern became obvious: Perplexity cited its pricing page repeatedly, ChatGPT never once did.

Step 3: how do you read AI referral traffic in your own analytics?

Most analytics platforms show a referral source for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and similar domains, provided you know to filter for them, since they arrive as ordinary referral traffic rather than a labelled category. Build a saved segment for the major AI domains and check it monthly.

Ahrefs' study of 3,000 sites found AI chatbots currently account for a small share of total visits, around 0.12% of page views and 0.17% of visitors, but conversion quality on that traffic often runs higher than typical organic search, since someone arriving from an AI answer has usually already had their question partly answered.

Watch the trend line more than the absolute number. A site going from a handful of AI referrals a month to fifty is a genuinely useful early signal, even while the total remains a rounding error against organic search.

In GA4, a saved exploration filtering session source or medium for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com does this without any extra tooling. A Southampton-based retailer set this up in an afternoon and found AI referrals had quietly overtaken one of its five paid channels by volume.

Step 4: how do you benchmark your share of voice against competitors?

Run the same prompt list against your two or three named competitors and record who gets mentioned, how often, and in what order. This step turns a list of yes-or-no citations into an actual competitive picture.

A simple spreadsheet does this well: one row per prompt, one column per engine, one column per competitor, and a mention-count total. The BCC and University of Essex's March 2026 survey found 54% of UK SMEs now use AI in some form, and around 60% of those use it for content and knowledge work, so the competitors worth benchmarking against are very likely doing some version of this already.

Tom & Co analysis: tracking a single prompt across three named UK-available visibility tools costs roughly £1.42 to £3.01 a month, converted to sterling from each vendor's published pricing (July 2026).

Re-run the whole exercise monthly rather than once. Share of voice in AI answers moves as engines recrawl the web and as competitors publish new content, and a single snapshot tells you where you stand today, not whether you are gaining or losing ground.

Weight the prompts that matter most. A Belfast-based professional services firm scored each prompt one to three for commercial value before benchmarking, so a single high-value comparison prompt counted for more than three low-value brand-awareness ones in the final share-of-voice score.

Step 5: how do you turn the gaps into a content plan?

Every prompt where a competitor gets cited and you do not is a content brief, not a mystery. Read what the AI engine actually quoted from the competitor's page, and check whether your own site answers the same question as directly and as early.

The fix is usually the one that also helps ranking in ChatGPT specifically: a citable answer paragraph near the top of the page, a dated statistic backing the claim, and a genuine comparison table where the topic calls for one.

Do not expect an overnight shift. Rewritten pages typically take two to six weeks to surface in AI answers, based on how often engines recrawl, so treat the first full monthly cycle as a baseline rather than a verdict on whether the changes worked.

An Exeter-based manufacturer turned its worst-performing prompt, a comparison question where three competitors were named and it was not, into a single rewritten page with a proper answer paragraph and a sourced statistic. The same prompt cited the manufacturer for the first time five weeks later.

Assign one content brief per week rather than trying to fix every gap at once. A prompt list of twenty typically surfaces four or five real gaps worth acting on, and fixing one properly beats rewriting five pages badly in the same afternoon.

Which tools actually do this, and what do they cost in the UK?

None of the five steps above strictly need a paid tool. Manual prompt testing and a spreadsheet cover all five for a small brand watching a handful of prompts. A paid tracker earns its keep once the prompt list grows past what one person can realistically check by hand every week.

What a paid tracker actually buys you is scale and consistency: the same prompt list run daily across several engines, without a team member remembering to do it by hand, plus a dashboard for the share-of-voice comparison from step 4 rather than a manually updated spreadsheet.

Engine coverage varies by vendor, which matters more than the headline price. Otterly and Peec both track ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode as standard, with Gemini available as an add-on. Semrush's toolkit leans toward Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode specifically, since it sits inside a platform built around Google data in the first place.
