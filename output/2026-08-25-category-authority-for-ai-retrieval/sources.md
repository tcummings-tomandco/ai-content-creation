# Sources: How do you build category authority so AI tools retrieve your brand?

Topic ID: 30 | Cluster: Marketing & Sales | Slug: category-authority-for-ai-retrieval

## Primary sources cited inline

1. **Ofcom, "Passive social media use, AI companionship, and online side hustles: UK adults' media and online lives revealed"** (part of the Adults' Media Use and Attitudes 2026 research, published 2 April 2026)
   URL: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/passive-social-media-use-ai-companionship-and-online-side-hustles-uk-adults-media-and-online-lives-revealed
   Full report PDF: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/adults/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-2026/adults-media-use-and-attitudes-2026-report.pdf?v=415430
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
   Claims supported: 54% of UK adults use an AI tool (up from 31% the previous year); age split of 79% (16-24) vs 18% (65+); 57% of adults aware of AI say they'd trust an AI-written summary less than a human-written one; 75% of online adults read AI-generated search summaries at least sometimes; 94% of UK adults have home internet access. Survey base: 7,533 UK adults aged 16+, fieldwork 29 September to 28 November 2025.

2. **Aggarwal, Murahari, Rajpurohit, Kalyan, Narasimhan, Deshpande, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"** (Proceedings of the 30th ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining, KDD '24)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
   Claim supported: content modifications (adding citations, statistics, and named expert attribution) can lift a page's citation rate in generative-engine responses by up to 40%, tested across a 10,000-query, 8-domain benchmark and validated on Perplexity.

3. **Competition and Markets Authority, "Digital comparison tools market study"** (final report published 26 September 2017)
   URL: https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/digital-comparison-tools-market-study
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
   Claim supported: UK price/product comparison sites (a category including Compare the Market, MoneySuperMarket, GoCompare, Confused.com) were the subject of a year-long CMA market study which found them heavily used and, on balance, positive for UK consumer outcomes. Cited to establish comparison sites as an already-regulated, trusted category of UK consumer intermediary, not for a specific usage percentage.

4. **British Chambers of Commerce and University of Essex (with Atos), "Half of SMEs using AI, with limited headcount impact so far"** (March 2026)
   URL: https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/03/half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far/
   Accessed: 2026-08-25 (previously accessed 2026-07-23 for stat_012)
   Claim supported: underlies the existing Tom & Co stat_012 calculation (2.1 million UK businesses now using AI for marketing or content work), reused here from data/stat_bank.json rather than recalculated.

5. **techUK** (UK technology trade association)
   URL: https://www.techuk.org
   Accessed: 2026-08-25
   Claim supported: used as the named example of a UK technology trade body whose membership signals third-party-vetted category membership.

## Sources considered and rejected

- Semrush, Similarweb and BotRank "most-cited domains" studies: useful directional colour (Reddit, Wikipedia, G2 dominate LLM citations) but not fetchable/verifiable as primary sources within this run, and not central to the UK-specific claims made in the article, so no statistic from them was cited inline.
- Statista brand-awareness figures for GoCompare/Confused.com: not on the approved primary-source domain list, so not cited.
- Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026 (AI chatbot use for news): relevant context but tangential to the category-authority argument, and not on the approved domain list, so left out rather than cited as a stretch.
