# Sources: What is the AI hallucination risk for UK businesses?

1. **Harber v Commissioners for HMRC [2023] UKFTT 1007 (TC)** (First-tier Tribunal, Tax Chamber, 4 December 2023)
   https://www.bailii.org/uk/cases/UKFTT/TC/2023/TC09010.html
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: the litigant cited nine fictitious tribunal decisions generated by an AI tool; the tribunal found she had used them unwittingly and dismissed her appeal against a £3,265 HMRC penalty.

2. **Ayinde, R (On the Application Of) v The London Borough of Haringey [2025] EWHC 1040 (Admin)** (High Court, Ritchie J, 3 April 2025)
   https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Ayinde-v-LB-Haringey-Judgment-Ritchie-J-03.04.25-HD-2.pdf
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: five fabricated case citations, including a fake Court of Appeal decision, in the claimant's pleadings; £2,000 wasted costs order against the solicitor and the barrister each; referral to the SRA and Bar Standards Board.

3. **Hamad Al Haroun v Qatar National Bank QPSC & Anor [2025] EWHC 1588 (Comm)** (High Court, Commercial Court, 26 June 2025)
   https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2025/1588
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: of 45 case-law citations relied on in an £89.4m damages claim, 18 did not exist; the client admitted using unchecked AI-generated material; the solicitor was referred to the SRA.

4. **Divisional Court judgment on Ayinde v Haringey and Al-Haroun v Qatar National Bank [2025] EWHC 1383 (Admin)** (6 June 2025)
   https://assets.caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/d-7aeb3b4d-6402-4119-a642-62a696cc3acc/d-7aeb3b4d-6402-4119-a642-62a696cc3acc.pdf
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: both matters were heard together under the court's Hamid jurisdiction (the mechanism for lawyers' basic duties to the court); the court warned that similar future conduct could amount to contempt.

5. **Law Gazette (The Law Society) — "Judge refers solicitors to SRA over inexcusable submission of AI-generated authorities"** (reporting a county court appeal hearing, March 2026)
   https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/judge-refers-solicitors-to-sra-over-inexcusable-submission-of-ai-generated-authorities/5126797.article
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: a further 2026 case in which citations submitted by two solicitors, including one linked to an unrelated immigration matter, appeared AI-generated and unverified; both were referred to the SRA.

6. **Solicitors Regulation Authority — "SRA approves first AI-driven law firm"** (press release, 6 May 2025)
   https://news.sra.org.uk/news/news/press/2025-press-releases/garfield-ai-authorised/
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: the SRA authorised Garfield.law, whose legal services are delivered solely by an AI-powered litigation assistant, to help businesses recover debts up to £10,000 through the small claims process.

7. **Solicitors Regulation Authority — "SRA updates supervision guidance to aid litigation work"** (12 June 2026)
   https://www.sra.org.uk/news/news/releases/supervision-guidance-updated
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: updated 24-page supervision guidance, developed with the Law Society, CILEX Regulation and the Legal Aid Agency, requiring AI-assisted output to receive the same authorised human review as work by a trainee or paralegal.

8. **Courts and Tribunals Judiciary — "Artificial Intelligence (AI) Guidance for Judicial Office Holders"** (updated 31 October 2025, replacing the April 2025 version)
   https://www.judiciary.uk/guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence-ai-judicial-guidance-october-2025/
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: the judiciary's own AI guidance expands on hallucination risk, warns against relying on AI-generated summaries in place of the underlying evidence, and flags "white text" manipulation risk.

9. **Information Commissioner's Office — "Guidance on AI and data protection"**
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/
   Accessed: 2026-08-04
   Supports: developers should assess and communicate hallucination risk; techniques such as retrieval-augmented generation reduce but do not eliminate it, since LLMs generate text from patterns rather than verified fact.

10. **Financial Conduct Authority — "Rethinking regulation for the age of AI"** (speech by Nikhil Rathi, Chief Executive, 24 June 2026)
    https://www.fca.org.uk/news/speeches/rethinking-regulation-age-ai
    Accessed: 2026-08-04
    Supports: validating AI outputs and preventing hallucinations remains an open challenge for firms and regulators; misinformation risk is a 2026 supervisory priority alongside algorithmic collusion; the AI Live Testing programme tests firms' systems under regulatory oversight.

## Original Tom & Co calculation (Layer 2)

**£800 in direct wasted costs per fabricated citation in the Ayinde v Haringey judgment.**

Workings: the court ordered the solicitor and the barrister to each pay Haringey £2,000 in wasted costs (source 2), a combined £4,000. Five fabricated cases were identified in the claimant's pleadings. £4,000 / 5 = £800 per fabricated citation, counting only the wasted costs the judge quantified. This excludes Haringey's own legal team's time spent identifying and investigating the fake citations, the SRA and Bar Standards Board referral process, and any reputational cost, all of which sit on top of this figure. Full workings also recorded in `stat_bank_update.json`.

Caveat: this is a single-case calculation, not an average across the four cases in the comparison table, since only Ayinde's judgment quantifies a wasted costs figure in the reporting reviewed. It should be read as an illustrative floor, not a typical cost.

## Sources considered and not used

A widely repeated claim that a dedicated "AI hallucination case tracker" website counts roughly 60 to 64 UK and international court matters involving fabricated AI citations was found during research (naturalandartificiallaw.com). It was not cited in the article: the site is an independent legal-commentary tracker rather than a court, regulator or disclosed-methodology research body, and its running count could not be independently verified against primary judgments within this research pass. The four cases cited in the article body are each backed by a primary judgment or an SRA-adjacent report and are sufficient to support the trend described. A "Work AI Index UK 2026" survey (Work AI Institute at Glean, reported via IT Pro and The Register, June 2026) on time spent verifying AI output ("botsitting") was also reviewed but left out, as the publisher is a commercial AI vendor rather than a primary UK regulator or a research firm on the approved list.
