# Sources: How are UK creative and media agencies using AI in 2026?

1. **DSIT / Sally Davies, AI Champion for the Creative Industries — "AI Adoption Plan: Creative Industries"**
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-champions-ai-adoption-plans/ai-adoption-plan-creative-industries
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: 51% of UK creative industries businesses use AI vs 33% economy-wide; sub-sector split of 60% IT/software, 53% design and designer fashion, 44% film/TV/video/photography, 22% music and performing arts. Published 8 June 2026.

2. **Office for National Statistics — "Artificial intelligence in UK businesses: 2023 to 2026"**
   https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/businessservices/articles/artificialintelligenceinukbusinesses/2023to2026
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: 58% of Information and Communication sector businesses used at least one AI technology by June 2026 (highest of any UK sector); national average rose from ~12% (late 2023) to ~35% (mid-2026).

3. **DCMS — "DCMS Economic Estimates: GVA 2023 (provisional)"**
   https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/dcms-economic-estimates-gva-2023-provisional-data-tables-only
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: UK creative industries GVA of £124bn in 2023 (~5% of UK GVA); IT, software and computer services £49.1bn (nearly 40% of the total); ~2.4 million jobs (~7% of UK jobs) in the year to June 2024. Published 19 December 2024.

4. **ISBA generative AI survey, reported by Marketing Week — "Marketers' use of generative AI quadruples in one year, ISBA finds"**
   https://www.marketingweek.com/marketers-gen-ai-isba/
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: advertisers running at least one live gen-AI use case rose from 9% (April 2024) to 41% (July 2025); 62% cite efficiency as the primary driver; 27% experimenting, 28% exploring. Reported 9 September 2025.

5. **IPA — "IPA Agency Census 2025"**
   https://ipa.co.uk/news/agency-census-2025
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: IPA member agencies employed 24,963 people on 1 September 2025 (down 6.8% YoY); creative/non-media agency employment fell 14.3%, media agency employment grew 2.4%; 88.3% of agencies say AI has a considerable impact on their work; 8% cut headcount in the past 12 months directly due to AI, 24% expect to in the next 12 (30% among creative/non-media agencies vs 10% media agencies); 10% of agencies have revised contracts for gen-AI, 37% mid-revision. Disclosed methodology: online census among 201 corporate member agencies (~88% of the IPA's estimated employed base), fielded Sept-Nov 2025.

6. **ASA / CAP — "AI, advertising, and the policy landscape: CAP proactive monitoring" and "Disclosure of AI in Advertising"**
   https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ai-advertising-and-the-policy-landscape-cap-proactive-monitoring.html
   https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: existing, media-neutral Advertising Codes apply in full to AI-generated or AI-assisted advertising content, with no AI-specific exemption; ASA reported handling a growing number of AI-related complaints through 2025; no blanket UK legal requirement to disclose AI use in ads, though misleading-impression risk still applies.

7. **UK Intellectual Property Office / gov.uk — "Copyright and artificial intelligence: statement of progress under Section 137, Data (Use and Access) Act"**
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-progress-report/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-statement-of-progress-under-section-137-data-use-and-access-act
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: the copyright and AI consultation ran 17 December 2024 to 25 February 2025 and drew more than 11,000 responses; the statement of progress, published 15 December 2025, sets out three reform objectives (control, access, transparency).

8. **Courts and Tribunals Judiciary — "Getty Images -v- Stability AI" [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch)**
   https://www.judiciary.uk/judgments/getty-images-v-stability-ai/
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: judgment handed down 4 November 2025; Getty's secondary copyright infringement claim dismissed; trade mark claim under section 10(3) dismissed; "extremely limited" findings under sections 10(1) and 10(2) for early Stable Diffusion versions only. (Independently corroborated via multiple UK law-firm case notes: Bird & Bird, Latham & Watkins, Squire Patton Boggs, Mayer Brown, given direct fetch of judiciary.uk was blocked by this session's network policy.)

9. **DCMS — "£380 million boost for creative industries to help drive innovation, regional growth and investment"** and Hansard, "Industrial Strategy: Creative Industries Sector Plan" (23 June 2025)
   https://www.gov.uk/government/news/380-million-boost-for-creative-industries-to-help-drive-innovation-regional-growth-and-investment
   https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2025-06-23/debates/25062337000010/IndustrialStrategyCreativeIndustriesSectorPlan
   Accessed: 2026-08-11
   Supports: £380m Creative Industries Sector Plan, published 23 June 2025; £25m for 5 new CoSTAR satellite labs plus 2 demonstrator spaces; doubled funding for the Creative Industries Clusters programme; £200m for regions outside London; ambition to grow business investment in the sector from £17bn to £31bn by 2035; expanded British Business Bank support commitment.

## Original Tom & Co calculation (Layer 2)

**Nearly 40% of the UK creative industries' £124bn GVA (2023) sits within IT, software and computer services, the sub-sector that also has the highest AI adoption rate (60%) of any part of the creative industries (2026).**

Workings: DCMS's Economic Estimates GVA 2023 (provisional) puts IT, software and computer services at £49.1bn of the creative industries' £124bn total GVA (£49.1bn / £124bn = 39.6%, rounded to "nearly 40%"). The AI Adoption Plan for the Creative Industries (DSIT, June 2026) separately reports 60% AI adoption for that same sub-sector, the highest of any creative-industries sub-sector measured. Cross-referencing the two shows the most AI-exposed part of the creative industries is also its single largest contributor to sector GVA by value.

Caveat: the GVA figure (2023) and the adoption figure (2026) are not contemporaneous measurements. This is presented as a structural/directional observation about where AI adoption and economic value currently overlap in the sector, not a claim that AI adoption caused or explains the GVA figure. Full workings recorded in `stat_bank_update.json`.

## Sources considered and not used

Innovate UK's "Next Wave: Breakthrough" and "Collaborative R&D: Creative Catalyst" competition pages were reviewed as possible funding-line citations, but their project delivery windows (one ran to 31 March 2026) had already lapsed by the time of writing (August 2026) and could not be confirmed as still open, so they were left out rather than presented as live opportunities. The £380m Creative Industries Sector Plan and its CoSTAR/Creative Industries Clusters funding lines are multi-year commitments and were used instead. A subagent's earlier research pass also surfaced sub-sector GVA figures for "advertising and marketing" (~£21.5bn) and "film, TV, video and photography" (~£21.2bn); the advertising and marketing figure could not be independently re-verified against a resolvable DCMS table reference in this session (network access to gov.uk's underlying data tables was blocked), so it was not used in the article or the Layer 2 calculation, which relies only on the IT/software/computer services figure that was independently corroborated via the House of Lords Library's briefing citing the same DCMS table.
