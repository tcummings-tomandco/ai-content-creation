# Sources: Will AI take my job? What the UK data actually shows in 2026

All accessed 2026-09-22.

1. **ONS, Labour market overview, UK: June 2026**
   https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/bulletins/uklabourmarket/june2026
   Claim supported: UK unemployment rate at 4.9% in the three months to April 2026, down 0.3pp on the previous quarter.

2. **British Chambers of Commerce and Atos (with the University of Essex ESRC Centre for Micro-Social Change), "Half of SMEs Using AI - With Limited Headcount Impact So Far", March 2026**
   https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/03/half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far/
   Claims supported: 54% of UK SMEs actively using AI in 2026 (up from 35% in 2025, 25% in 2024, 23% in 2023); 95% of SME AI users report no headcount change in the past year, 86% report no change to job roles; larger SMEs and B2B professional services firms lead adoption, smaller/consumer-facing/manufacturing firms lag. Also the basis for the Layer 2 Tom & Co calculation (see stat_bank_update.json).

3. **DSIT (AI and Future of Work Unit), "Assessment of AI capabilities and the impact on the UK labour market", January 2026**
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessment-of-ai-capabilities-and-the-impact-on-the-uk-labour-market/assessment-of-ai-capabilities-and-the-impact-on-the-uk-labour-market
   Claims supported: hiring has fallen faster in more AI-exposed occupations, but the unit states whether AI is responsible "remains unclear"; ~70% of UK workers are in occupations containing AI-exposed tasks, split 35% high-exposure/high-complementarity, 32% high-exposure/low-complementarity, 33% low-exposure.

4. **"How Exposed Are UK Jobs to Generative AI? Developing and Applying a Novel Task-Based Index" (GAISI), arXiv 2507.22748**
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22748
   Claims supported: 94% of UK jobs show some LLM exposure by 2023/24, only 13% heavily exposed (GAISI score >0.5), concentrated in scientific/technical professions; wage premium for AI-exposed tasks fell 12% between 2017 and 2023/24; relative contraction in job postings for more AI-exposed occupations since ChatGPT's release; 24% (7.4 million) of UK workers reported using AI-enabled software at work by Q2 2024.

5. **Greater London Authority, "London's workforce exposure to generative artificial intelligence", April 2026**
   https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-04/London%E2%80%99s%20workforce%20exposure%20to%20generative%20artificial%20intelligence.pdf
   Claims supported: routine administrative and clerical roles top the exposure ranking, with ICT, finance, professional services and public administration close behind; explicit caveat that high exposure "does not signal definitive job losses, nor does low exposure guarantee job and occupational security".

6. **Bouke Klein Teeselink (King's College London), "Generative AI and Labor Market Outcomes: Evidence from the United Kingdom", SSRN**
   https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5516798
   Claims supported: firms highly exposed to generative AI cut total headcount by 4.5% on average (2021-2025 difference-in-differences design); junior roles at those firms fell 5.8%; high-paying, AI-exposed firms cut employment 9.6% against near-zero change at low-paying firms; job postings for high-salary occupations fell 34.2%.

7. **PwC UK, 2026 AI Jobs Barometer**
   https://www.pwc.co.uk/services/technology/generative-artificial-intelligence/uk-ai-jobs-barometer.html
   Claim supported: UK postings for specialist AI roles rose 61% to 179,954 in 2025, up from around 112,000 in 2024.

8. **King's College London Policy Institute / King's Institute for Artificial Intelligence, UK attitudes to AI and the Future of Work survey (Opinium fieldwork, 16-29 April 2026, launched at the King's AI Summit: Workforce Futures, 19-20 May 2026)**
   https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/one-in-five-britons-think-ai-will-create-civil-unrest-study-finds
   Claim supported: seven in ten UK workers are worried about AI-related job losses.

9. **DSIT and Skills England, "AI Skills for Life and Work: Labour market and skills projections", January 2026**
   https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-labour-market-and-skills-projections/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-labour-market-and-skills-projections
   Claim supported: share of UK job adverts requiring AI skills reached 2.2%.

## Note on access method

WebFetch to individual source pages was blocked by this session's network egress policy (all outbound domain fetches returned EGRESS_BLOCKED). All figures above were sourced and cross-checked via multiple independent WebSearch queries per claim, using the search engine's synthesised summaries of the cited pages. Each figure was corroborated by at least one search pass explicitly attributing it to the named report/page before being used in the article. Where a figure could not be independently corroborated to the author's satisfaction (e.g. a specific British Progress Institute finding on computer programming sector employment), it was deliberately left out of the article rather than used on single-source confidence.
