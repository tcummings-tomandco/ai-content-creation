# Sources: What's the lightest AI governance framework for a UK SME?

Topic ID: 42 | Cluster: Risk, Governance & Legal | Slug: ai-governance-for-smes-lightest-framework

## Primary sources cited inline

1. **gov.uk, Guidance for using the AI Management Essentials tool**
   URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/ai-management-essentials-tool/guidance-for-using-the-ai-management-essentials-tool
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: AIME consultation ran 6 November 2024 to 29 January 2025; the self-assessment questionnaire's three thematic areas (internal processes, managing risks, communication); AIME's stated basis in ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

2. **gov.uk, Guidance for using the AI Management Essentials tool: government response**
   URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/ai-management-essentials-tool/outcome/guidance-for-using-the-ai-management-essentials-tool-government-response
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: DSIT's December 2025 decision not to publish AIME or make it a procurement requirement; 65 consultation responses analysed; feedback that a size-and-role-agnostic tool didn't serve very different organisations; DSIT's plan to develop future SME-focused foundational guidance.

3. **ICO, Governance and accountability in AI**
   URL: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/audits/data-protection-audit-framework/toolkits/artificial-intelligence/governance-and-accountability-in-ai/
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: the ICO's UK GDPR accountability framework applies to AI; requirement for a named DPO or nominated data protection lead accountable for AI risk, with day-to-day oversight from a steering group, committee or equivalent; risk register requirements linking corporate and departmental risk to AI systems.

4. **gov.uk, Trusted third-party AI assurance roadmap**
   URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/trusted-third-party-ai-assurance-roadmap/trusted-third-party-ai-assurance-roadmap
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: DSIT's planned AI Assurance Platform as a one-stop shop for assurance tools and services; the AI Essentials Toolkit planned specifically for start-ups and SMEs; the AI Assurance Innovation Fund opening for applications in spring 2026 with £11 million.

5. **British Chambers of Commerce (with Atos), Half of SMEs Using AI, With Limited Headcount Impact So Far** (March 2026)
   URL: https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/03/half-of-smes-using-ai-with-limited-headcount-impact-so-far/
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: 54% of UK firms actively using AI by March 2026, up from 35% in 2025, 25% in 2024 and 23% in 2023; around 94% of surveyed firms were SMEs; +71 percentage point net productivity gain expected by current AI users over the next year.

6. **The Law Society, Compliance and the use of AI in law firms**
   URL: https://communities.lawsociety.org.uk/risk-and-compliance/compliance-and-the-use-of-ai-in-law-firms/6003325.article
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: recommendation for a named senior individual with AI oversight, a cross-functional committee, and regular audits feeding the firm's risk register; SRA data showing around 75% of the UK's largest law firms use AI against roughly 30% of small firms still exploring it.

7. **FCA, AI and the FCA: our approach**
   URL: https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: the FCA's reliance on the existing Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR) and Consumer Duty, rather than new AI-specific rules, to govern firms' use of AI; senior manager accountability for effective control of AI use.

8. **FCA, Review into the long-term impact of AI on retail financial services (the Mills Review)**
   URL: https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/calls-input/review-long-term-impact-ai-retail-financial-services-mills-review
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: the FCA's ongoing long-term review into AI's impact on retail financial services, used to support the point that today's principles-based approach may firm up further.

9. **gov.uk (MHRA), Software and artificial intelligence (AI) as a medical device**
   URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device/software-and-artificial-intelligence-ai-as-a-medical-device
   Accessed: 2026-09-08
   Claim supported: AI performing or supporting a diagnostic or treatment function can be classified by the MHRA as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), triggering a separate registration and risk-classification regime.

10. **gov.uk, AI Opportunities Action Plan: One Year On** (Tom & Co Layer 2 calculation, reused from `data/stat_bank.json` stat_001, first calculated 2026-04-29)
    URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-opportunities-action-plan-one-year-on/ai-opportunities-action-plan-one-year-on
    Accessed: 2026-09-08
    Claim supported: roughly £2.6 billion in direct UK government AI funding announced or detailed across the twelve months covered by the Action Plan, including assurance and adoption-support funding lines.
